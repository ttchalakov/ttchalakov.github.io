Teodor Tchalakov

Closing the gap between physical intelligence and its fundamental limits.

I’m an M.S. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, where I’m part of the Reliable Autonomy Group and a teaching assistant for ECE 486, Control Systems.

Robots are learning remarkable skills, but there are limits no robot can learn its way past. Some are physical: a robot’s dynamics decide what is feasible, from a high-level plan down to a single motor command. Others are about complexity: how much a robot must sense, compute, and represent to succeed. I want to understand these fundamental limits, and build physical intelligence that reasons about them and learns to reach them. Right now, that means studying how little information a controller needs to keep a system safe, and deploying resource-efficient learned policies on nano-drones.

Research Interests

  • How much intelligence does a physical task require? Making effective decisions can require a minimum amount of information and computation. I’m interested in characterizing these fundamental limits on decision-making, to scope the intelligence a robot needs for a task.
  • How should a robot’s dynamics and environment inform its decisions? The limits that matter include underactuated or hard-to-model dynamics, limited communication, and environments that change while the robot acts. I’m interested in robots that know their limits and can act flexibly within them.
  • How can a robot learn compact representations for physical tasks? I’m interested in new learning problems where the limits of the task, the robot, and its environment shape the model’s architecture, and in analyzing how the resulting policies are trained and how they perform.

Publications

  1. Under review
    Task Entropy for Low Bitrate Robot Control: Minimal data rate controller for a nano-drone
    Teodor Tchalakov and Sayan Mitra
    2026
    Abs

    Low bitrate robot control requires knowing how much feedback information a task demands. Reachability entropy answers this for reaching a fixed target set, but visiting locations in order or returning to the start depends on the execution history, since one physical state can occur at different task stages. We introduce task entropy for tasks given as labels on executions, extending reachability entropy to history-dependent tasks with no automaton fixed in advance. For set-valued dynamics with full-state observations, we prove that it equals the least worst-case average data rate of coder–controllers guaranteeing completion. We learn a coder–controller that refines the set of states consistent with the sent symbols only when needed. On two history-dependent nano-drone tasks it reduces the maximum bit count 2.3 to 5.6 times relative to a fixed-grid coder in simulation, and Crazyflie nano-drone flights demonstrate the communication scheme on hardware.

  2. CDC
    Minimal Information Control Invariance via Vector Quantization
    Ege Yuceel, Teodor Tchalakov, and Sayan Mitra
    In IEEE Conference on Decision and Control (CDC), 2026
    To appear
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Safety-critical autonomous systems must satisfy hard state constraints under tight computational and sensing budgets, yet learning-based controllers are often far more complex than safe operation requires. To formalize this gap, we study how many distinct control signals are needed to render a compact set forward invariant under sampled-data control, connecting the question to the information-theoretic notion of invariance entropy. We propose a vector-quantized autoencoder that jointly learns a state-space partition and a finite control codebook, and develop an iterative forward certification algorithm that uses Lipschitz-based reachable-set enclosures and sum-of-squares programming. On a 12-dimensional nonlinear quadrotor model, the learned controller achieves a 157× reduction in codebook size over a uniform grid baseline while preserving invariance, and we empirically characterize the minimum sensing resolution compatible with safe operation.

  4. ICRA-W
    Automatically-Tuned Model Predictive Control for an Underwater Soft Robotic Arm
    David Null, Teodor Tchalakov, William Edwards, Kris Hauser, and Y Z
    In ICRA 2022 Workshop on Compliant Robot Manipulation, 2022
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    Soft robot arms are especially appealing candidates for underwater manipulation thanks to their actuation mechanisms. However, modeling and controlling soft arms to achieve precision tasks has remained a significant challenge. Learned MPC has been applied to soft robot control in prior work, but we address the problem that MPC is highly sensitive to the hyperparameters of the learned model and optimizer, and tuning them manually is a tedious process. In this work, we adopt the AutoMPC approach to automatically tune the MPC controller from an offline dataset, and extend it to handle multi-task tuning. On a 2D, hydraulically actuated underwater soft robot, we compare this method to a learned inverse kinematics model that predicts the actuation pressures needed to achieve a static configuration. Although both controllers were trained on the same dataset, AutoMPC achieved 75% improvement in targeting accuracy.

Working Papers

  1. RISS
    Slack Convex Feasible Set Trajectory Optimizer
    Teodor Tchalakov, Simin Liu, Changliu Liu, and John M. Dolan
    Working paper, Robotics Institute Summer Scholars (RISS), Carnegie Mellon University, 2024
    Abs Bib PDF Video Poster

    Trajectory planners are a fundamental robotics tool where often highly nonlinear and non-convex problems are formulated to solve for high-quality trajectories with respect to nonlinear system dynamics, obstacle avoidance, and other general constraints. The optimization-based trajectory planning approach seeks to find locally optimal trajectories in real time that are feasible. The Slack Convex Feasible Set (SCFS) algorithm was proposed to handle non-convex inequality and nonlinear dynamics equality constraints in real time by convexification. We present SCFS as a general purpose kinodynamic trajectory optimizer and derive a new convexification method for compact non-convex feasible sets. However, we observe that for problems with large negative Hessian eigenvalues and small feasible regions, the convergence rate and initialization robustness of SCFS suffer significantly. We demonstrate methods to improve initialization robustness and assess methods for improving convergence rates on a dynamic conveyor picking problem.

  2. RISS
    Guaranteed Parameter Uncertainty Estimation Using Interval Analysis for Robust Safety-Critical Control
    Teodor Tchalakov, Qin Lin, and John M. Dolan
    Robotics Institute Summer Scholars Working Papers Journal, vol. 10, Carnegie Mellon University, 2022
    Abs Bib PDF Video Poster

    Robust control Lyapunov functions (CLFs) and control barrier functions (CBFs) use the largest bounds of parameter uncertainties to achieve robust safety-critical control, but the literature has proposed the bounds be manually tuned. This tuning problem requires balancing large uncertainty bounds in robust controllers that produce aggressive behavior against small uncertainty bounds that violate robustness. We present an over-approximate model parameter uncertainty estimation method for applications in robust CLF-CBF quadratic programming (QP) controllers using interval analysis. This work expands upon the set inversion via interval analysis (SIVIA) algorithm, which can be used offline to find bounds on parameter uncertainties from system observations. We improve the efficiency of SIVIA for finding the largest verified parameter intervals with an over-approximate heuristic search that uses system identification to provide initial guesses. The identification of model uncertainties can enable the lifelong operation of robust safety-critical systems. We test our approach’s accuracy and efficiency in comparison to SIVIA on an adaptive cruise control scenario with a robust CLF-CBF-QP controller.
